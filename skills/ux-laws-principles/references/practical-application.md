# Practical UX application by product and journey

## Navigation and architecture
**Related references:** [Jakob’s Law and Hick-Hyman](ux-laws-and-effects.md); [Information Foraging, Pirolli & Card (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.106.4.643); [Gestalt](principles-heuristics-gestalt.md).
- **What:** Organization of content, features and paths.
- **Why:** People need to predict where to find something.
- **Who:** Visitors, new users and experts.
- **Where:** Menus, categories, breadcrumbs, search and footer.
- **When:** Internal labels, redundant paths or hard-to-find pages.
- **How:** Organize by tasks and mental models; use clear labels; show location; test findability.
- **Concepts:** Jakob, Hick-Hyman, Information Foraging, Gestalt.

## Onboarding and activation
**Related references:** [Fogg (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1145/1541948.1541999); [COM-B, Michie et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42); [Cognitive load, Sweller (1988)](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4).
- **What:** Experience that leads to the first meaningful result.
- **Why:** Completed sign-up does not mean perceived value.
- **Who:** New users.
- **Where:** First session, registration, setup and first task.
- **When:** Early abandonment or long time to first value.
- **How:** Explain value; ask only for necessary data; show real progress; allow resume; define activation as an outcome.
- **Concepts:** Fogg, COM-B, Cognitive Load and progressive disclosure.

## Forms
**Related references:** [Fitts’s Law, original study](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0055392); [Nielsen heuristics](principles-heuristics-gestalt.md).
- **What:** Collection, validation and review of information.
- **Why:** Ambiguity and errors increase effort.
- **Who:** Everyone, especially novices and people providing sensitive data.
- **Where:** Sign-up, checkout, briefing and configuration.
- **When:** Frequent errors, misunderstood fields or abandonment.
- **How:** Persistent labels; explain sensitive data; examples; contextual validation; preserve data; group fields.
- **Concepts:** Fitts, error prevention, recognition and feedback.

## Search and filters
**Related references:** [Information Foraging, Pirolli & Card (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.106.4.643); [recognition and visibility-of-status heuristics](principles-heuristics-gestalt.md).
- **What:** Mechanisms to locate and refine content.
- **Why:** Reduce search space without losing context.
- **Who:** Users of catalogs, knowledge bases, dashboards and marketplaces.
- **Where:** Search, filters, sorting and results.
- **When:** Too many results, zero results or confusing filters.
- **How:** Clarify scope; show active filters; allow clear; preserve query; explain empty states.
- **Concepts:** Information Foraging, recognition and visibility of system status.

## Dashboards
**Related references:** [Gestalt and Nielsen heuristics](principles-heuristics-gestalt.md); [cognitive load, Sweller (1988)](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4).
- **What:** Presentation of indicators for decision-making.
- **Why:** Data without context increase effort and risk of misinterpretation.
- **Who:** Managers, operators and analysts.
- **Where:** Dashboards, reports and panels.
- **When:** The user cannot identify what requires action.
- **How:** Organize by decision; show unit, period, comparison and source; do not use color as the only signal.
- **Concepts:** Gestalt, Prägnanz and Cognitive Load.

## Checkout, subscription and critical actions
**Related references:** [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); [FTC, Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light); [OECD, Dark Commercial Patterns](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/dark-commercial-patterns_44f5e846-en.html).
- **What:** Financial commitment or hard-to-reverse action.
- **Why:** Ambiguity about cost and consequence harms trust.
- **Who:** Buyers, subscribers and users of critical actions.
- **Where:** Payment, renewal, upgrade, deletion and cancellation.
- **When:** Before commitment or irreversible action.
- **How:** Show total price, periodicity, fees and terms; allow review; confirm critical actions; make cancellation findable.
- **Avoid:** False urgency, hidden costs and obstructive cancellation.

## Mobile and touch
**Related references:** [Fitts (1954)](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0055392); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).
- **What:** Experience on small screens and touch.
- **Why:** Space, precision and context differ from desktop.
- **Who:** Mobile users and people with motor limitations.
- **Where:** Navigation, forms, menus and gestures.
- **When:** Whenever there is mobile use.
- **How:** Adequate targets; comfortable reach; do not rely on hover; alternatives to drag; test virtual keyboard, zoom and reflow.
- **Concepts:** Fitts, Steering and WCAG.

## Notifications
**Related references:** [Fogg (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1145/1541948.1541999); [COM-B, Michie et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42); [Self-determination, Deci & Ryan (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68).
- **What:** Messages that draw attention to events.
- **Why:** Interruptions have a cost.
- **Who:** Users with important events and tasks.
- **Where:** Push, email, SMS and in-product notifications.
- **When:** Actionable and relevant information.
- **How:** Explain the reason; allow channel and frequency control; do not use guilt or false urgency.
- **Concepts:** Fogg, COM-B and Self-Determination.

## Empty, loading and error states
**Related references:** [Nielsen heuristics](principles-heuristics-gestalt.md); [Doherty & Thadhani (1982)](https://www.ibm.com/support/pages/system/files/inline-files/EconomicValueofResponseTime.pdf).
- **What:** States that communicate absence, processing, success or failure.
- **Why:** The person needs to know what happened and how to proceed.
- **Who:** Everyone, especially in asynchronous operations.
- **Where:** Lists, dashboards, upload, generation and search.
- **When:** On every relevant transition.
- **How:** Differentiate states; use real progress; explain what was preserved; offer safe recovery.
- **Concepts:** Visibility of system status, Doherty and error recovery.

## Plan selection
**Related references:** [Hick-Hyman](ux-laws-and-effects.md); [Iyengar & Lepper (2000), choice overload](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.995); [Von Restorff](ux-laws-and-effects.md).
- **What:** Comparison and choice of offer.
- **Why:** Unclear differences make decision harder.
- **Who:** Buyers with varied needs.
- **Where:** Pricing and upgrade.
- **When:** The user needs to identify the suitable option.
- **How:** Compare relevant dimensions; explain limits; justify recommendations; do not hide limitations.
- **Concepts:** Hick-Hyman, Similarity, Von Restorff and autonomy.

## Microcopy
**Related references:** [Nielsen heuristics](principles-heuristics-gestalt.md); Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things* (2013), for conceptual models and signifiers.
- **What:** Short texts that guide actions and explain states.
- **Why:** Language influences interpretation and trust.
- **Who:** Everyone, especially non-technical audiences.
- **Where:** Buttons, fields, instructions, errors and confirmations.
- **When:** It is unclear what to do or what will happen.
- **How:** Audience vocabulary; concrete verbs; explicit result; recovery without blaming.
- **Concepts:** Real world, mental models, recognition and signifiers.

## Ethical conversion
**Related references:** [FTC, Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light); [OECD, Dark Commercial Patterns](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/dark-commercial-patterns_44f5e846-en.html); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).
- **What:** Design that helps evaluate value and complete the appropriate action.
- **Why:** Sustainable conversion depends on clarity and trust.
- **Who:** Visitors, leads and buyers.
- **Where:** Landing pages, pricing, checkout and forms.
- **When:** There is a real decision.
- **How:** Specific value proposition, verifiable evidence, clear price and fair comparison; preserve freedom of choice.
- **Avoid:** False scarcity, artificial timers, hidden costs, confusing opt-out, forced continuity and fabricated social proof.

Higher conversion does not prove better UX. Monitor refunds, complaints, cancellations, errors and user success.
