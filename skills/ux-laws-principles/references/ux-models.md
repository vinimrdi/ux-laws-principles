# Cognitive, behavioral and experience models

Models simplify reality. Choose the one that answers the question; do not force every product into a single framework.

## Mental model and conceptual model
**Reference:** Norman, D. A., *The Design of Everyday Things* (2013, revised edition). Conceptual reference for mental models, conceptual models and interaction.
- **What:** A mental model is the user’s internal understanding; a conceptual model is the representation communicated by the product.
- **Why:** Divergence generates error and surprise.
- **Who:** New users and people in complex processes.
- **Where:** Architecture, naming, onboarding and navigation.
- **When:** Behavior seems arbitrary or requires constant explanation.
- **How:** Research expectations, observe tasks and align language, structure and behavior.

## Don Norman’s seven stages of action
**Reference:** Norman, D. A., *The Design of Everyday Things* (2013, revised edition). The seven stages and the gulfs of execution/evaluation are presented and developed in that work.
- **What:** Form goal, intention, specify action, execute, perceive state, interpret and evaluate result.
- **Why:** Identifies failures between intention and understanding of the result.
- **Who:** Teams analyzing interactions and critical flows.
- **Where:** Save, publish, pay, search and edit.
- **When:** The person does not know what to do, cannot execute, or does not understand the result.
- **How:** Improve purpose and discoverability; make controls operable; provide feedback and confirmation.
- **Gulf of execution:** Distance between intention and available actions. **Gulf of evaluation:** Distance between system state and ability to understand it.

## Cognitive load theory
**Reference:** [Sweller (1988), Cognitive load during problem solving: Effects on learning](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1202_4); [Cowan (2001), review of short-term memory capacity](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922).
- **What:** Demand on working memory during learning and problem solving; intrinsic, extraneous and germane load are often distinguished.
- **Why:** Unnecessary effort competes with comprehension.
- **Who:** Novices and people in complex tasks.
- **Where:** Onboarding, forms, dashboards and instructions.
- **When:** The user gets lost, rereads or switches screens.
- **How:** Remove duplication, group, give examples and keep instructions next to the action.
- **Limit:** Category terminology varies in the literature.

## Information foraging theory
**Reference:** [Pirolli & Card (1999), Information Foraging](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.106.4.643). Model of informational search and navigation; applications to labels and information architecture are design adaptations.
- **What:** People use cues to estimate where to find information and how much effort it will cost.
- **Why:** They compare probable value and cost of exploration.
- **Who:** Users of sites, catalogs and knowledge bases.
- **Where:** Navigation, search, categories and links.
- **When:** They cannot find information or abandon exploration.
- **How:** Informative labels, categories in the user’s language and paths without dead ends.

## Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)
**Reference:** Christensen et al., *Competing Against Luck* (2016), practical synthesis of the framework. JTBD has different schools and formulations; there is no single academic definition. Investigate situations and alternatives in real research.
- **What:** People “hire” solutions to make progress in a situation; the job can be functional, emotional and social.
- **Why:** Features do not explain context and desired outcome.
- **Who:** Strategy, product, research and positioning.
- **Where:** Discovery, value proposition and prioritization.
- **When:** The team does not understand why someone would choose the product.
- **How:** Investigate trigger, progress, alternatives and outcomes in real experiences.
- **Limit:** Does not replace research and evidence.

## Fogg Behavior Model (B=MAP)
**Reference:** [Fogg (2009), A Behavior Model for Persuasive Design](https://doi.org/10.1145/1541948.1541999); [Fogg Behavior Design Lab, Stanford](https://behaviordesign.stanford.edu/resources/fogg-behavior-model).
- **What:** Behavior occurs when motivation, ability and a prompt converge.
- **Why:** Intention is not enough; the person must be able to act and notice a stimulus.
- **Who:** Teams investigating completion and adoption.
- **Where:** Onboarding, activation, forms and notifications.
- **When:** Intention exists but the action does not happen.
- **How:** Reduce friction if ability is low; clarify relevance if motivation is low; offer a contextual prompt if the trigger is missing.
- **Ethics:** Do not exploit vulnerabilities.

## COM-B model
**Reference:** [Michie, van Stralen & West (2011), The behaviour change wheel](https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42). Paper that presents the COM-B model within the Behaviour Change Wheel.
- **What:** Behavior depends on capability, opportunity and motivation.
- **Why:** Action can fail due to lack of skill, external condition or disposition.
- **Who:** Behavior-change products and recurring tasks.
- **Where:** Digital health, education, productivity and services.
- **When:** Reminders do not solve non-completion.
- **How:** Identify the barrier and choose a compatible intervention.

## Self-determination theory
**Reference:** [Deci & Ryan (2000), The 'What' and 'Why' of Goal Pursuits](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68). Presents basic psychological needs and self-determined motivation.
- **What:** Highlights autonomy, competence and relatedness as important psychological needs.
- **Why:** Choice, mastery and connection can favor higher-quality motivation.
- **Who:** Learning, collaboration, productivity and community.
- **Where:** Personalization, feedback, goals and collaboration.
- **When:** Sustainable motivation is sought.
- **How:** Offer meaningful choices and feedback that builds competence; avoid coercion.

## Goal-setting theory
**Reference:** [Locke & Latham (2002), Building a Practically Useful Theory of Goal Setting and Task Motivation](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705). Theoretical review on goal setting and performance.
- **What:** Specific goals can support performance when accepted and accompanied by ability, resources and feedback.
- **Why:** Clear goals orient attention and effort.
- **Who:** Users in tasks with measurable outcomes.
- **Where:** Productivity, learning and projects.
- **When:** There is a concrete objective and reasonable control.
- **How:** Help define the outcome, give feedback and allow goal adjustment.

## User journey map
**Reference:** There is no single seminal paper that standardizes all journey maps. Treat as a research-synthesis artifact; document method, participants, data and the distinction between facts and hypotheses.
- **What:** Stages, goals, actions, touchpoints, emotions and difficulties.
- **Why:** Problems occur before, during and after the interface.
- **Who:** Product, service, support and operations.
- **Where:** Acquisition, contracting, use, support and renewal.
- **When:** Experience crosses channels or teams.
- **How:** Map real stages and evidence; do not invent emotions.

## Service blueprint
**Reference:** [Bitner, Ostrom & Morgan (2008), Service Blueprinting](https://doi.org/10.1177/1094670508314262). Paper on blueprinting as a technique for service innovation and improvement.
- **What:** Relates customer actions, visible interactions, internal processes, systems and evidence.
- **Why:** Experience depends on operations invisible to the user.
- **Who:** Services with support, logistics, payments and integrations.
- **Where:** Finance, health, education, e-commerce and support.
- **When:** The interface does not fix the root cause.
- **How:** Connect customer stages to frontstage and backstage processes.

## Double Diamond
**Reference:** [Design Council, The Double Diamond](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/) and [history of the model](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/history-of-the-double-diamond/). Institutional source of the framework.
- **What:** Discover, Define, Develop and Deliver.
- **Why:** Separates problem exploration from solution exploration.
- **Who:** Multidisciplinary teams.
- **Where:** Discovery, redesign and new products.
- **When:** The initial request may not represent the real problem.
- **How:** Explore, synthesize, generate alternatives, prototype and test.
- **Limit:** Not a mandatory linear process.

## Design Thinking
**Reference:** [Brown (2008), Design Thinking](https://hbr.org/2008/06/design-thinking). Professional synthesis of the approach; not a single scientific protocol or mandatory process.
- **What:** People-centered approach with understanding, definition, ideation, prototyping and testing.
- **Why:** Broadens perspectives and allows learning from prototypes.
- **Who:** Teams facing ambiguous problems.
- **Where:** Research, workshops and innovation.
- **When:** There is uncertainty about problem and solution.
- **How:** Combine real research with prototypes and test cycles.

## HEART
**Reference:** [Rodden, Hutchinson & Fu (2010), Measuring the User Experience on a Large Scale](https://doi.org/10.1145/1753326.1753687). Origin work of the HEART framework.
- **What:** Metrics framework: Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention and Task Success.
- **Why:** Translates UX goals into observable signals.
- **Who:** Product and analytics.
- **Where:** Metrics and evaluation of changes.
- **When:** Business metrics do not explain experience quality.
- **How:** Choose relevant dimensions and define goals, signals and metrics.
- **Limit:** More engagement or retention is not automatically better.

## AARRR
**Reference:** AARRR/Pirate Metrics is a practical growth framework, often associated with Dave McClure. There is no universal scientific definition; cite the material and version used in each application.
- **What:** Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral and Revenue.
- **Why:** Organizes growth analysis across the customer lifecycle.
- **Who:** Digital businesses and growth teams.
- **Where:** Acquisition, onboarding, recurrence, referral and monetization.
- **When:** Mapping growth bottlenecks.
- **How:** Define events and cohorts, connect metrics to user value and monitor collateral damage.

## Quick choice
Interaction: Seven Stages of Action. Overload: Cognitive Load. Findability: Information Foraging. Context: JTBD. Behavior: Fogg or COM-B. Autonomy: Self-Determination. Journey: Customer Journey Map. Operations: Service Blueprint. Exploration: Double Diamond. UX metrics: HEART. Funnel: AARRR with guardrails.
